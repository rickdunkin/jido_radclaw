## Determination (TL;DR)

**Nothing to adopt as a dependency; the haul is the attention layer — and unlike every prior ades dig, part of it is worth building *now*, before argus.** CCC is the only project in the set that seriously answered "which of my N agents needs me, *including the ones that ended mid-question*": a two-stage prose soft-block detector layered over formal signals, an `ended_blocked` state for dead sessions with an orphaned question, a 10-kind priority-ranked attention feed with suppression rules, and git-stranded-work kinds (uncommitted/unpushed after the agent stopped). All of it is scraping-fed there — and read-model-fed here, because the seams pass found the embarrassing half of the story on our side: **three attention signals already exist in jido_radclaw and reach no operator surface at all** (LoopGuard halts go to the LLM + telemetry only; failed cron runs land in DB + logs; Forge `:needs_input` broadcasts have zero operator-facing subscribers, and `Runner.blocked/1` has zero producers while the CLI runners classify a final assistant question as `:done`). The scan's other headline candidate deflates on contact: the mtime-409 editable-node model is a **half-precedent** — the server contract is real and convergent with our `stale_revision` design (typed conflict carrying the fresh cursor), but the celebrated client recovery flow was never built (409 dead-ends; recovery loses the operator's edits), which converts the borrow into a design *requirement* for argus §5.4 rather than a working reference. The trust posture is the sharpest cautionary entry in the corpus (CSRF-allowlist-≠-auth, an Origin-less-request bypass of even their "localhost-only" config endpoint, ungated GET over transcripts, and skills that instruct agents to bypass their own sandbox to drive the control plane). Worktree-init, PWA shell, and the live layer contribute small confirming datapoints to decisions traycer/emdash already shaped.

**Why not adopt as a dependency**: (1) architecture is the deliberate inverse of ours on every axis (no DB vs event-sourced Postgres, rescan-and-heuristics vs durable projections, loopback-single-user vs authed tailnet cluster); (2) the detection machinery — the hardest-engineered part — is exactly the part our structured events make unnecessary (the scan's rule holds: borrow taxonomy and UX, never detection plumbing); (3) on the orchestration axis the asymmetry runs our way, as with traycer and emdash: no durable events, no gate family, no leases — the *attention* layer is the one axis where CCC is ahead of us, and it's a pattern, not a dependency.

## Tier 1 — load-bearing now

### CC1-1. Prose soft-block detection + the `ended_blocked` state

**Recommendation**: BORROW-PATTERN + BORROW-RUBRIC (the scoring table verbatim as the starting point). The most distinctive idea in the subject, and the one the scan flagged correctly.

**Where in CCC**: `_detect_soft_block(c)` (`server.py:51087-51128`) is two-stage. Stage one short-circuits on **formal** signals (`question_waiting` — an in-flight `AskUserQuestion`; `needs_approval` — a blocking permission notification) as `guaranteed=True`, score 99. Stage two runs only when the session is *not working*: a terminal gate requires no pending tool, zero in-flight sub-agents, no mid-write sidecar, last event not from the user, and non-empty final assistant text (`:51111-51121` — proven by `tests/test_attention_detector.py:90-121`: working/pending/mid-write/user-replied sessions are never flagged). Then `_score_soft_block` (`:51050-51084`) scores the **trailing 700 chars** of the prose (their own `<session-state>` scaffolding and prompt trailer stripped first, `:51034-51040`): ends-with-`?` **+3**; else `?` in last 400 chars **+1**; closing-intent phrase hits **+2 each, capped +4** (`_SOFT_BLOCK_PHRASES`, `:50956-50969` — ~50 phrases: "want me to", "shall i", "should i proceed", "your call", "waiting on you", "for your review", "sound good", "sign off", "wdyt", …); an enumerated list **and** a choice word together **+2** (`:50972-50980,51072`); threshold **score ≥ 3**. Emitted with `reasons` and an extracted `question_text` — deliberately transparent, "NOT a black box" (`:50948-50950`). The same detector applied to a *dead* session yields **`ended_blocked`** (`_session_ended_blocked`, `:51191-51197`): process gone, question orphaned — a sub-flag on `ended`, not a fifth state. Their docs record why this exists: on 14 live sessions, ~7-8 needed the human and the formal flags caught **zero** (`docs/attention-api.md:11-14`). Two doc/code drifts for the record: the doc says phrases are "+2 each" (code caps at +4) and omits the choice-word conjunct on the enum signal.

**What**: A cheap, transparent, test-pinned answer to the failure mode formal gates structurally cannot catch — the agent *asked in prose* and nothing fired. Detection runs only at rest (never on a working agent), scores only the tail, and grades its own confidence (`guaranteed` vs scored).

**Gap in jido_radclaw** (verified 2026-07-03): **nothing scans assistant final text for blocked-shape anywhere** — the only content classifiers are the front door's *inbound* triage (`reasoning/triage.ex:33`) and LoopGuard's tool-*result* classification (`agent/loop_guard.ex:259-274`). The concrete misses, per the seams pass: Forge's ClaudeCode runner reduces stream-json to `continue | done` — **a final assistant question is classified `:done`** (`forge/runners/claude_code.ex:115-149`; Codex likewise, `codex.ex:176-309`); `Runner.blocked/1` exists in the runner contract with **zero producers**, and the harness has no `:blocked` arm — it would fall through to `:ready` (`forge/runner.ex:16,61-70`; `forge/harness.ex:660-687`); a spawned sub-agent that ends by asking a question just records `:done` and waits for a `get_agent_result` pull (`conversations/subagent_transcript.ex:212-213`, `tools/spawn_agent.ex:226-234`); a cron-triggered run's final question lands silently in the DB (`platform/cron/worker.ex:210-262`). Our formal-blocked authority — child runs `:awaiting_approval` via `WorkflowView.put_gate_block/4` (`orchestration/workflow_view.ex:280-299`) — is exactly CCC's stage one and nothing more.

**Why it matters**: This set of gaps exists *today*, before argus. Non-interactive surfaces (cron, composer waves, spawned workers, Forge sessions) are precisely where the operator isn't watching, and "ended with an unanswered question" is indistinguishable from "done" on every surface we have. It also composes with, not duplicates, next-ten #6: camus C1-5 detects *finding-level* stalls inside fix loops (fingerprint repeats), CC1-1 detects *conversational* blockage at turn/run boundaries — different signals, same inbox (CC1-2).

**Adoption sketch**: (a) A pure `JidoClaw.Attention.SoftBlock` module — `score/2` over final assistant text, phrase table adapted from CCC's (MIT; keep the port-attribution comment style of `LoopGuard`), same shape: formal short-circuit / working-state gate / tail scoring with reasons. Our working-state gate reads `AgentTracker` + in-flight signals instead of sidecars. (b) Run it at terminal boundaries: `SubagentTranscript.record_result`, the composer/reactor terminal event append, cron worker completion, and the Forge runners' `parse_output` — where a scored hit finally gives `Runner.blocked/1` its first producer (add the missing harness arm rather than the silent `:ready` fallthrough). (c) Store the classification as metadata on the terminal event/session (a `soft_block` map with score/reasons/question_text), don't gate on it — it feeds the CC1-2 read-model; whether it ever *opens* a case is OQ-1. (d) Keep their at-rest discipline: never classify a running agent; a live turn ending in a question on an interactive surface (REPL/Discord) is normal conversation, exempt. (Connective note, 2026-07-03: the same-day muxara dig supplies the test method for this adoption — a calibration corpus of real captured final-text fixtures, categorized by intended classification, pinned by table tests and retained for recalibration, with the port-drift lesson attached: port the tests with the mechanism; [MX3-1](../muxara/FEATURES-WORTH-BORROWING.md), a natural `JidoClaw.Eval` fit.) (Cross-corpus note, 2026-07-05: two pms datapoints for the `ended_blocked` family — multica's considered anti-interactive stance (`AskUserQuestion` disabled, clarifications forced into issue comments + a severity inbox) is the field's purest producer of "the agent ends by asking in prose" ([multica dig](../../pms/multica/FEATURES-WORTH-BORROWING.md)); and symphony ships the input-required taxonomy with the park-don't-retry rule — blocked sessions leave the dispatch pool until reconciliation releases them ([SY2-2](../../pms/symphony/FEATURES-WORTH-BORROWING.md)) — which its own fork then deleted, the cautionary for treating blocked-surfacing as optional.)

### CC1-2. The attention feed as a read-model — kinds, priority, suppression, machine consumers

**Recommendation**: BUILD-ON (ours to design over projections; CCC supplies the taxonomy and the consumer model) — with an INDEPENDENT do-today slice: three of our signals are operator-invisible now.

**Where in CCC**: `_classify_attention(c)` (`server.py:51208-51446`) folds every session into at most one attention item with a `kind` and priority 1–7: `question_blocked` / `stale_tool_call` / `pending_tool` (P1), `sidecar_waiting` / `soft_block` (P2), then the **git-stranded-work kinds** — `pushed_open` (P3, pushed but PR still open), `uncommitted_edits` (P4, session dead with edits never committed), `committed_not_pushed` (P5, verified against actual unpushed commits) — then backlog kinds (P6-7). **Suppression is half the value**: archived/verified rows never flag (`:51221`); a dormant session whose structured `next_step` starts with a WAIT/DONE prefix is dropped (`:51236-51250`); the cross-repo feed additionally keeps an idle-waiting row only if the soft-block detector fires or the structured next-step *requests a decision* (`_awaits_human_decision`, `:51020-51029` — drops loop-workers idling between tickets). The feed (`compute_attention_feed`, `:51554-51754`) is priority-then-recency sorted, caps enrichment at 40 items, excludes `committed_not_pushed` by default, and drops non-P1 items older than 48h. Consumer model: the feed is explicitly **"plumbing for the COO sweep — infrastructure only, no opinion/recommendation/auto-answer layer; the live COO agent consumes these feeds and makes the calls"** (`docs/attention-api.md:1-4`) — the decider (human or agent) is outside the system. A machine-facing SSE variant exists (`/api/sessions/events`, `server.py:50399-50479` — id/state/flags deltas keyed on a 3-tuple), with an honestly documented blind spot: prose-only soft-blocks don't reach SSE (`:4244-4247`).

**What**: Attention as a *derived, prioritized, suppressed* feed over existing state — not a notification firehose. Two ideas travel: the kind taxonomy (blocked > stalled > **stranded work** > backlog) and the deliberate split between producing the feed and deciding what to do about it.

**Gap in jido_radclaw** (verified 2026-07-03): our operator-attention inventory is pending-gate counts and pull surfaces, full stop — dashboard's Pending Approvals stat card (`web/live/dashboard_live.ex:36-40`), `/approvals` + REPL `/gates`, id-only `orchestration:gates` pokes (`orchestration/run_pubsub.ex`, `orchestration/tool_approvals.ex:294-297`), and MCP `agent_status`/`workflow_status` pulls. Meanwhile three real signals surface **nowhere an operator looks**: (i) LoopGuard halts exist only as the LLM's error envelope + Trace/telemetry/logs — no PubSub, no dashboard card (`agent/loop_guard.ex:170-171,433-449`); (ii) a failed cron run is a log line + DB row + auto-disable after 3 strikes — no notification path exists at all, and Discord *cannot* carry one today (`Nostrum.Api.Message.create`'s only caller is the inbound reply path, `platform/channel/discord.ex:55,64-75`; `platform/cron/worker.ex:210-262`); (iii) Forge `:needs_input` broadcasts go to a per-session topic with zero operator-facing subscribers — both LiveViews subscribe to the sessions topic only (`forge/harness.ex:661-663`, `forge/pubsub.ex:11-22`, `web/live/forge_live.ex:9`, `dashboard_live.ex:16`). The REPL status line carries no gate/attention signal either (`cli/presenters.ex:35-76`).

**Why it matters**: Argus §6.2 (push triggers) and the SPA's badge layer both need a *source*; emdash EM1-3 answered "which events push," and this entry is the layer under it — "what is the set of needs-you items right now," queryable and suppressed, so pushes and badges read one model instead of growing ad-hoc queries. The stranded-work kinds map directly onto argus-era worktrees (a converged run leaving a dirty checkout / unpushed branch is attention, not history). And the do-today slice is just honesty: signals we already emit should reach the surfaces we already have.

**Adoption sketch**: (a) **Today, no argus dependency**: an `Attention` view module (pure reads: pending `AgentCase`s, `:awaiting_approval` runs via `put_gate_block`, LoopGuard halted keys from `LoopGuard.Store`, recent failed/auto-disabled cron jobs, Forge sessions parked `:needs_input`) surfaced as an MCP tool (`attention_status`, the `workflow_status` precedent) and a dashboard card/list; wire `dashboard_live` to the existing broadcasts it already subscribes to. (b) With CC1-1: add `ended_blocked` items (terminal run/session + question_text). (c) Suppression from day one, our way: gate on run/case state rather than text where possible; adopt the WAIT/DONE-prefix idea only if/when worker structured outputs grow a `next_step` field (OQ-2). (d) At argus steps 2-3: expose the same model as a GraphQL field + channel topic; EM1-3's two push triggers become "P1 items entering the feed," and the priority ranking is the per-severity mute knob EM OQ-3 wanted. (e) Keep the COO split: the feed never auto-answers; a future supervisor agent consuming it is just another client (our MCP surface already fits — and unlike CCC's, ours sits behind auth and the tool-approval gate). ### CC1-3. The 409 editable-node contract — a half-precedent that sharpens argus §5.4

**Recommendation**: BORROW-REFERENCE for the server half; the client half is a **cautionary correction** to the scan and a design requirement for argus step 4. (Convergence credit stays with CCC; the mechanism on our side is traycer TR2-3's `expected_seq` check.)

**Where in CCC**: Flow node saves (`_flow_save_node_payload`, `server.py:8167-8202`) implement optimistic concurrency on file mtime: client sends the mtime it loaded (`app.js:14665-14672`); server compares against disk under a 1ms tolerance and returns **`409 {"ok": false, "error": "flow file changed on disk", "mtime": <current>}`** — carrying the fresh cursor in the conflict body (`:8191-8195`). Writes are atomic (temp + `os.replace`), size-capped (1 MiB), path-clamped. That's the mature half. The immature half, verified against the client: **the recovery flow was never built.** The save handler has no status-code branch — a 409 renders the raw error string and dead-ends (`app.js:14679-14683`); the stale mtime is never refreshed, so retry loops 409 forever; the only recovery (re-opening the inspector) replaces the textarea with server content, **losing the operator's edits**; and the "Refresh auto sections" action bypasses the check entirely (`overwrite=True`, `server.py:8219`) and clobbers unsaved edits (`app.js:14704-14720`). Their own design doc promised "Reload and Overwrite without silently losing edits" (`docs/flow-workspace.md:279-281`) — unimplemented. Two more contract honesty notes: a falsy/absent client mtime silently degrades to last-write-wins (`server.py:8190`), and their System-A objects store has no optimistic concurrency at all (merge + last-writer-wins per field, `objects_store.py:468-561`).

**What**: Independent convergence on the shape argus sketched — *typed conflict carrying the current cursor, client refetches and re-edits* — **plus** a worked demonstration that the conflict response is the easy 20% and the recovery UX is the feature. A 409 nobody can act on is functionally last-write-wins with extra frustration.

**Gap in jido_radclaw** (verified 2026-07-03): unchanged from traycer TR2-3, which owns the mechanism: no revision machinery exists; the event append is pessimistic (`FOR UPDATE` in `orchestration/workflow_event/changes/allocate.ex:171-195`), and `expected_seq` is a small net-new check inside that lock returning a typed `stale_revision` error with the current seq. This entry adds the requirements the CCC read hardens.

**Why it matters**: Argus §5.4's `reviseStepOutput(expectedSeq)` cites "the CCC mtime-409 flow" as its recovery model. That flow half-exists. If we inherit only the server half — as CCC did — the phone operator who took 30 seconds too long to edit a step output loses the edit, on the exact surface (mobile review) argus exists to serve.

**Adoption sketch**: fold into the argus step-4 design as acceptance criteria, not code: (a) `stale_revision` carries current seq **and** the current head revision ref, so the client can refetch without a second round trip; (b) the client keeps the operator's buffer authoritative across the refetch — re-render the diff against the new base, never replace the textarea (the exact CCC failure); (c) no unchecked bypass: absent `expectedSeq` is a validation error, not last-write-wins; any force-overwrite is an explicit, logged mutation argument (TR3-1's honesty pairing); (d) machine rewrites of reviewable artifacts go through the same check — CCC's `overwrite=True` refresh path is the anti-pattern. (Cross-corpus note, 2026-07-05: the acceptance-criteria list this entry started is now five axes plus one end-to-end proof, assembled by the pms digs ([pms observation 9](../../pms/README.md)) — **approve-fence**: Chorus's approve→materialize has no concurrency fence, double-approve double-materializes ([CH1-1](../../pms/chorus/FEATURES-WORTH-BORROWING.md)), and orca's Accept ships the same defect; **revision history**: Chorus keeps a single overwritten `reviewNote`; **durability**: bosun's resumed gates re-open to pending even when already approved; **expiry**: OpenHelm's pending approvals never expire (the XA2-1 convergence); **timeout direction**: bosun defaults `onTimeout:"proceed"` — the argument for the inverted house rule that a gate timeout only ever fails closed. The end-to-end proof is myrlin's severed-consumer lesson: its spinoff editor promotes the operator's edit verbatim into a record **no agent ever consumes**, so §5.4 must prove the resumed step consumes the head revision's bytes at its input, not merely that the revision was stored ([MY2-1](../../pms/myrlin-workbook/FEATURES-WORTH-BORROWING.md)). Plan-layer promote-the-edit itself now exists three times in the field (Chorus, orca's Accept, myrlin's spinoff) — the §5 novelty claim narrows to execution-layer head-promotion, still unclaimed at 24 subjects.)

## Tier 2 — adopt at the named argus step (one INDEPENDENT)

### CC2-1. Attention UX rules: stable order, badges, hoist only the orphaned questions, anti-flap hysteresis

**Recommendation**: BORROW-RUBRIC (argus step 2's UI) — the second shipped datapoint confirming emdash EM2-1, plus two rules emdash doesn't have.

**Where in CCC**: CCC *removed* attention-sorting after shipping it — the tombstone is explicit: *"the 'Needs you' SECTION is gone. Pulling every waiting session up into a top bucket made rows jump out of their project group and back the instant a turn paused/resumed — the constant churn the user called out"* (CCC-182, `app.js:20625-20632`). What remains: stable recency order with an order-hysteresis band (`:21734-21742`), a blinking in-row "needs you" badge (`:21511-21515`), a dedicated kanban `waiting` column fed by formal flags (`:18488,18661-18662`), and exactly **one** hoisted bucket — `ended_blocked` sessions from the last 48h (`_openAskConvs`, `:20633-20643`). Anti-flap is layered and deliberate: demote-only stickiness on `waiting` (enter instantly, hold exits ~90s while live — CCC-174, `server.py:3279,3283-3308`), a fresh-session sticky pinning new rows until real progress (`app.js:18499-18531`), and 5-minute ordering hysteresis on the triage band to survive a 5s poll (`:22540-22571`). Hard signals still outrank everything in *classification* (approval/stale-tool beat manual column overrides, `:18478-18488`) — rank ≠ reorder.

**What**: Three rules for a live agent list: (1) never reorder on attention — badge in place; (2) the one exception worth hoisting is *dead with an unanswered question*, because there's no live row to badge; (3) state transitions are debounced asymmetrically — alarms raise instantly, clear slowly.

**Gap in jido_radclaw** (verified 2026-07-03): EM2-1's gap stands (no fold, no seen-flag); our agent list statuses have no needs-input concept (`agent_view.ex:28-29,59-64` — `:awaiting_handoff` banner is the nearest thing, `web/live/agents_live.ex:61-65`), and nothing anywhere debounces status flap because nothing live-updates per-agent state yet.

**Why it matters / Adoption sketch**: With CCC-182, two products with real users independently shipped and kept badges-over-reordering (emdash never sorted; CCC sorted and retracted; the same-day termic dig made it three — badges on stable order, [TM2-5](../termic/FEATURES-WORTH-BORROWING.md)) — that settles the argus list design: EM2-1's fold + seen-flag, ordering stable, `ended_blocked` (CC1-1) as the single hoisted bucket, and the asymmetric-debounce rule applied client-side when the channel layer starts streaming per-agent transitions. One sentence of doctrine in the SPA's first list component; nearly free because our transitions are durable events, not poll flicker. (Same-day muxara dig: the corpus's one *shipping* sorter demonstrates the churn cost concretely — its positional selection ring drifts onto a different session across reorders — and contributes the corollary rule: selection and focus bind to identity, never grid position; [muxara MX2-1](../muxara/FEATURES-WORTH-BORROWING.md). Its state-level damping — a 5-minute working→idle cool-off, alarms instant — is a third convergent datapoint for the raise-fast/clear-slow rule, applied at the state layer where CCC damps ordering.)

### CC2-2. Managed-block co-ownership (`ccc:auto`) — re-homed to our `system_prompt.md` sync pain

**Recommendation**: INDEPENDENT (surfaced by this dig; worth doing regardless of argus) — the pattern is CCC's, the application is ours.

**Where in CCC**: `_flow_managed_block` wraps machine-owned regions in `<!-- ccc:auto:start <name> -->` … `<!-- ccc:auto:end <name> -->` markers (`server.py:7925-7930`); regeneration replaces exactly the marked span (first match, case-insensitive) or appends the block if markers are absent — never touching prose outside (`:7933-7943`); a pinned test proves human text survives refresh (`tests/test_smoke.py:2722-2743`). Regeneration happens **only on explicit operator action**, never on load or poll (`:8139-8164`; `docs/flow-workspace.md:353-355`). Caveats recorded: section headings inside the markers are machine-owned (edits there are lost), and the refresh path's `overwrite=True` interacts badly with unsaved edits (CC1-3).

**What**: One document, two owners, mechanically separated: machine regions regenerate idempotently between markers; everything else is the human's, forever. The verify step is a regex, not a merge.

**Gap in jido_radclaw** (verified 2026-07-03): our nearest patterns are all copy-once-then-diverge: `.jido/system_prompt.md` is written only if absent (`agent/prompt.ex:52-58`), drift is *detected* via a machine-only SHA sidecar (`:355-363`) and a `.default` marker copy, and the offered fix — `/upgrade-prompt` — **replaces the whole file, discarding operator edits** (`cli/commands.ex:873`; the REPL nags until you choose, `cli/repl.ex:149`). AGENTS.md documents the resulting manual chore: "when tools or skills are added to the defaults, manually copy the updated default to `.jido/system_prompt.md`." `JIDO.md`/`config.yaml` are the same create-once shape (`platform/jido_md.ex:8-14`).

**Why it matters**: This is a real, standing operational annoyance with a known failure mode (operator customizations vs. default upgrades — pick one). Managed blocks dissolve it: the tool/skill inventory sections become machine-owned regions the upgrade path regenerates in place; operator guidance lives outside the markers and survives every upgrade. Same trick later applies to any generated-but-annotated doc (workflow catalog exports, argus-era status docs) — but `system_prompt.md` alone justifies it.

**Adoption sketch**: `JidoClaw.ManagedDoc` (~60 lines: `block/2`, `replace/3`, marker regex, append-if-absent, a survives-edit test); restructure `priv/defaults/system_prompt.md` with `<!-- jido:auto:start tools -->`-style regions; `/upgrade-prompt` becomes "regenerate managed regions, leave the rest" with the whole-file replace demoted to `--force`; the sync sidecar keeps working as the drift detector. Their heading caveat becomes a doc'd rule: headings live inside the machine region deliberately. (Connective note, 2026-07-03: the same-day Xantham dig supplies the second reference — a discipline package declares what it installs and how to verify it (the habits manifest), and its update posture is ff-only, halt-don't-merge, explicit consent for new capabilities (AUTOSYNC); [XA3-1](../Xantham-system-blueprint/FEATURES-WORTH-BORROWING.md).) (Cross-corpus note, 2026-07-05: pad supplies the counter-datapoint — its definition store deliberately freezes imported snapshots and *removed* its auto-upgrade hook after it hurt intentional divergence (IDEA-1479); our `system_prompt.md` problem sits mid-spectrum between pad's frozen-snapshot pole and this entry's managed blocks — cite [PD1-4](../../pms/pad/FEATURES-WORTH-BORROWING.md) in that design conversation.)

### CC2-3. Worktree datapoints: advisory init hook, disk-truth reads, and the no-teardown anti-pattern

**Recommendation**: FOLD-IN to the argus step-1 reading stack (traycer TR1-3/TR2-1/TR2-2 + emdash EM1-1/EM1-2 remain the primary references).

**Where in CCC**: created at spawn only — `<source-parent>/<repo>-wt/<slug>/` sibling layout, branch `feat/<slug>`, collision suffixes kept aligned between path and branch (`server.py:17150-17220`); creation failure aborts the spawn, no silent non-worktree fallback (`:30098-30099`). The init hook is **advisory**: `<worktree>/.ccc/worktree-init`, executable-only, env contract `CCC_WORKTREE_PATH`/`CCC_PARENT_REPO`/`CCC_SESSION_NAME`, cwd = worktree, 120s timeout, output appended to the spawn log, failures logged and **never block the spawn** (`:17223-17265`) — the deliberately-weak end of the spectrum whose strong end is traycer's six-state `setup_status`. Reads are disk-truth: `git worktree list --porcelain` + dirty flag + lock reasons + **per-branch open-PR match and `orphan_prs`** (`:39457-39552`). Teardown: **none** — the Merge button injects a prompt asking *the agent* to delete its own worktree and branch (`:49374-49383`).

**What / why it matters**: Three additions to the composed reference set: (1) the advisory-hook datapoint argues for TR1-3's separation — CCC proves a hook that can't fail the create is *worse* than no hook (a worktree whose setup failed still receives an agent; nothing records it — exactly what `setup_status`/`setup_exit_code` exist to prevent); (2) `orphan_prs` (a PR whose worktree/branch is gone locally) is a stranded-work attention kind worth adding to TR2-1's orphan sweep and CC1-2's taxonomy; (3) delegate-teardown-to-the-agent is the anti-pattern our gate family exists to forbid — a prompt-level `rm`+branch-delete with no busy-check, strengthening the case for emdash EM2-3 (gate `git worktree remove` in the shell analyzer) and TR2-1's phased delete. (Cross-corpus note, 2026-07-05: the pms corpus supplied both poles this anti-pattern sits between — orca force-deletes worktree *and branch* on reject/cancel/delete, rejected work unrecoverable; myrlin's record-delete strands checkout and branch with no reconciliation sweep ([pms observation 11](../../pms/README.md)) — and `orphan_prs` gained its shipped sweep: symphony's `before_remove` closes stranded open PRs at teardown, the only PR-side answer in either corpus ([SY2-4](../../pms/symphony/FEATURES-WORTH-BORROWING.md)).)

**Gap in jido_radclaw** (verified 2026-07-03): unchanged — no worktree functionality (`security/shell_command/git.ex:150` is still the sole textual hit); the argus §3.1 sketch plus the traycer/emdash entries carry the design.

### CC2-4. The tailnet trust posture — the corpus's sharpest negative reference for argus §4.4

**Recommendation**: ALREADY-COVERED (argus §4.4: API key on GraphQL + WebSocket via the existing `ApiKeyAuth`, tailnet ACLs at the network layer — `web/plugs/api_key_auth.ex:41,45`, `web/channels/user_socket.ex:10-32`). Take the garnish: three lessons for §4.4's review checklist. This corrects the scan's "no auth at all" in both directions.

**Where in CCC**: the posture is *documented honestly* (SECURITY.md: loopback trust boundary, "no auth — if you need multi-user access, fork and add it", spawned agents run `--dangerously-skip-permissions`, "anyone who can reach the API can run arbitrary commands as your user") — softer than the scan implied (localhost bind default, same-origin checks on every POST, list-form subprocess discipline, path-traversal checks). And sharper: the origin check is **CSRF defense, not authentication** — requests with **no Origin header pass every POST gate** (`server.py:46534-46535`), including `/api/network-config`, whose "a tailnet peer must not expand its own trust" guard runs only `if origin:` (`:46965-46982,47007-47012`) — so an Origin-less curl from any reachable peer can rewrite the trust config and restart the server, contradicting their own mobile-access spec's guarantee. **GET is entirely ungated** (`do_GET` routes immediately, `:44718-44735`): every transcript readable by any peer. `/api/term/run` is self-described in-code as "the most powerful endpoint in CCC… executes arbitrary shell as the user with no permission prompt" (`:44514-44519`). `CCC_TRUST_TAILNET=1` auto-adds every tailnet identity as a command-capable origin (`:7500-7579`). And the orchestration skill *instructs agents* to reach the API "with the network sandbox disabled (localhost IPC)" — agents are told to punch out of their own sandbox to drive the control plane that can spawn more agents.

**What / why it matters**: Under our threat model the relevant attacker is not an internet stranger — it's an LLM agent on a tailnet machine. CCC demonstrates the exact end state argus must not reach: a control plane where any agent with `curl` can read every transcript (leakage) and spawn/steer permission-less peers (misbehavior amplification). Three checklist lessons for §4.4: (1) **origin/CSRF allowlists authenticate nothing** — every surface, including WebSocket connect and any future SSE, must require the API key even for Origin-less programmatic clients; (2) **reads are not safe by virtue of being reads** — transcripts and step outputs carry secrets (our OutputRedaction exists for this; CCC's world-readable spawn logs are the counterexample), so GET/query surfaces sit behind the same key; (3) **config-mutation endpoints deserve a stricter tier than data mutations** (their network-config-rewrites-trust hole) — for argus, anything that changes auth/network posture stays off the GraphQL surface entirely. Nothing to build; fold the three lines into §4.4 when it lands. (Cross-corpus note, 2026-07-05: pad is the third §4.4 negative reference — its auth-perimeter wreck survey (a 4-perimeter email-verification retrofit, bearer-admin suppression, a 7-hole "restricted owner" fix family) shows what retrofitting a trust tier onto a shipped surface costs ([PD2-3](../../pms/pad/FEATURES-WORTH-BORROWING.md)); pms observation 5 re-confirms auth hygiene as the field's weak flank (symphony: none, localhost; myrlin: shared password + a pairing endpoint shipped broken) — this §4.4 posture survives a second corpus.)

## Bottom line

1. **The attention layer is the haul, and part of it is due now.** Three of our own signals are operator-invisible today (LoopGuard halts, cron failures, Forge needs-input) — CC1-2's read-model shape fixes that this week, and CC1-1's prose soft-block detector (rubric portable verbatim, MIT) closes the gap no formal gate can: runs that *end* by asking a question. `Runner.blocked/1` has been waiting for exactly this producer.

Client-side recover-without-losing-edits: never built — so argus §5.4 inherits acceptance criteria, not a reference implementation (CC1-3).

5. One sleeper: **managed-block co-ownership dissolves our `system_prompt.md` upgrade-vs-edits chore** (CC2-2) — a ~60-line module and a restructured default, independent of argus entirely.
